Disease-specific meta-analysis tools

We have extended this framework into a family of disease-specific meta-analysis platforms that systematically synthesize evidence on associations between nutritional exposures and human health outcomes. Although the individual platforms focus on different disease areas, they share a common evidence-synthesis framework, including automated literature screening and evidence extraction, study-level quality assessment, random-effects meta-analysis, forest plots, heterogeneity assessment, sensitivity analyses, and tools for interpreting pooled effect estimates. The disease-specific platforms also incorporate outcome definitions, subtypes, baseline incidence information, and analytical options tailored to each clinical domain.

Women's cancer meta-analysis tool: MetaFemina

MetaFemina was the initial application of this framework and focuses on associations between nutritional exposures and breast, ovarian, and uterine cancers. The platform allows users to examine individual nutritional exposures, incorporate predefined subexposure relationships, review the underlying studies, and obtain pooled estimates and accompanying diagnostic analyses.

The deployed website is available at https://metafemina.up.railway.app/.

Neurological disease meta-analysis tool: MetaNeuro

MetaNeuro extends the framework to neurological and age-related neurological outcomes. The platform synthesizes evidence on nutritional exposures in relation to outcomes such as age-related cognitive decline, Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, and sleep-related outcomes. As with MetaFemina, users can inspect the underlying evidence and evaluate pooled associations across different nutritional exposures and neurological outcomes.

The deployed website is available at https://metaneuro-production.up.railway.app/.

Gastrointestinal disease meta-analysis tool: MetaGut

MetaGut focuses on nutritional exposures and gastrointestinal, hepatobiliary, and related metabolic diseases. Its disease scope includes conditions such as metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD/NAFLD), gallstone disease/cholelithiasis, diverticular disease, and acute pancreatitis, together with additional gastrointestinal outcomes incorporated into the platform. The goal is to provide a unified interface for evaluating how dietary factors and nutritional supplements are associated with gastrointestinal disease risk.

The deployed website is available at https://metagut.up.railway.app/.

Urological disease meta-analysis tool: MetaUro

MetaUro applies the framework to urological and urinary-system diseases, including urological malignancies and their major disease subtypes. The platform is designed to synthesize evidence on nutritional exposures across outcomes such as bladder, kidney, renal, and urothelial cancers and related urological conditions. Disease- and subtype-specific analyses allow evidence to be evaluated at different levels of clinical resolution rather than treating all urinary-system outcomes as a single category.

MetaUro is part of the expanding platform family; its current public Railway deployment URL will be added here once the active endpoint is confirmed.

Respiratory disease meta-analysis tool: MetaBreath

MetaBreath focuses on nutritional exposures and diseases of the respiratory system. The platform includes evidence on respiratory cancers as well as selected chronic respiratory diseases and is designed to accommodate clinically important stratification variables, including smoking status when this information is available. This is particularly important for respiratory outcomes because baseline risks and exposure–disease associations may differ substantially between never, former, and current smokers.

The deployed website is available at https://metabreath.up.railway.app/.

Thyroid disease meta-analysis tool: MetaThyroid

MetaThyroid evaluates associations between nutritional exposures and thyroid diseases. The platform provides a systematic framework for studying nutrients and dietary factors that may influence thyroid function or thyroid disease incidence while allowing evidence to be examined separately across different thyroid outcomes.

The deployed website is available at https://metathyroid.up.railway.app/.

Skin and hair disease meta-analysis tool: MetaCutis et Capillus

MetaCutis et Capillus extends the framework to skin, hair, and related diseases, including skin cancers. The platform allows nutritional exposures to be evaluated against dermatologic and hair-related outcomes while preserving distinctions between disease incidence and other clinically relevant endpoints where appropriate.

The deployed website is available at https://metacutiscapillus.up.railway.app/.

Cardiovascular disease meta-analysis tool: MetaHeart

MetaHeart extends the evidence-synthesis framework to cardiovascular diseases. The planned disease scope includes major cardiovascular outcomes and clinically important subtypes, allowing nutritional exposures to be compared across cardiovascular endpoints rather than relying on a single composite definition of cardiovascular disease. The platform is also being designed to incorporate age-, sex-, and follow-up-specific baseline disease risks where suitable reference data are available.

MetaHeart is currently part of the expanding collection of disease-specific applications; a current public Railway endpoint is not listed here until the deployment URL is confirmed.

Prostate cancer and men's health meta-analysis tool: MetaVir

MetaVir applies the framework to prostate cancer and related men's health outcomes. It is designed to evaluate nutritional exposures in relation not only to disease incidence but also, where appropriate, to clinically important disease characteristics and prognosis. This provides a complementary cancer-focused resource alongside MetaFemina and MetaUro.

MetaVir is under active development, and its current public Railway deployment URL will be added once the active endpoint is confirmed.

Cross-cancer meta-analysis tool: metaOnco

metaOnco provides a higher-level, cross-cancer view of the evidence generated by the disease-specific platforms. Instead of examining a nutritional exposure within only one cancer type, users can select an exposure and compare the available evidence across multiple cancers. This makes it possible to identify associations that appear across several cancer sites as well as associations that may be specific to particular cancers.

The deployed website is available at https://metaonco.up.railway.app/.
